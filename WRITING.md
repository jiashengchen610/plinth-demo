# Writing guidelines

## Voice

Warm, playful and proud of the food. We talk like a host welcoming guests to the family table: friendly, a little cheeky, always respectful of the dishes and where they come from.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone |
|---|---|
| Menu and dish descriptions | Appetising and specific: ingredients, texture, region; no clichés like "exotic" or "oriental" |
| Booking and ordering | Friendly and direct: say what to do next |
| Festivals and specials | Celebratory and fun, with real context for the occasion |
| Errors | Calm and exact: what happened and how to fix it; no apologies |
| Cancellations | Clear about what happens, warm in closing |

## Interface copy rules

- Buttons start with a verb and say what happens: "Book a table", not "Submit".
- An action keeps its name through the flow: "Book a table" leads to "Table booked".
- Sentence case for all labels, headings and buttons.
- Error messages name the problem and the fix in one or two short sentences.
- Use numerals for numbers ("3 guests") and always show prices with the currency.
- Give dish names in Chinese and English when known; use the restaurant's own romanisation consistently.

## Terminology

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Book a table | Make a reservation, Reserve |
| Order | Purchase, Checkout (in buttons) |
| Dish | Item, Product |
| Menu | Catalogue |
| Chef's special | Featured item |
| Spicy | Hot (when it could mean temperature) |
| Sign in | Log in, Login |
| Cancel booking | Delete booking |

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do write from the guest's side: "your table", not "reservation record".
- Do mark allergens and spice level in words, not only icons.
- Don't use "exotic", "oriental" or fortune-cookie puns.
- Don't use exclamation marks in errors.
- Don't use filler like "simply" or "just".
